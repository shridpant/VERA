\section{Analytical null diagnostic and pivot}
The published LRPO temporal reward uses $\Delta=a-\mu b$ and
$R=\operatorname{clip}((|\Delta|-0.05)/0.15,0,1)\max(0,\Delta)$,
with $\mu=0.8$. Assignment is restricted to correct ordered outputs.
Our synthetic calculation gives the following values:

At equality, $\Delta=0.2a$; positive reward begins at $a>0.25$.
These are analytical inputs, not measured model outputs. The prose describes strict ordered outperformance. An additional implementation gate would eliminate these counterexamples; author code has not been inspected. This does not invalidate the reported benchmark results.

It does not establish model-wide invariance or semantic faithfulness. Next assess whether these differing explanations make incompatible event-order claims, using source-frame review and explicit claim labels; if reliable labels cannot be established, retain the negative feasibility outcome and stop this direction. Do not optimize prompts on these test windows.


\section{Qualitative explanation review and stopping decision}
All 16 reversed/shuffled versus ordered response pairs were reviewed as text. Five were exact matches; the remaining descriptions range from rephrasing and omission to changed event accounts. For example, RoadAccidents133 changes from a car--motorcycle collision to a white van causing a motorcycle crash. Shooting033 changes from carrying an object to running away from a house. These accounts may describe different compatible aspects of a scene; text alone does not establish a contradiction.

The review is a post-hoc Codex assessment, not independent human annotation. No source frames were inspected for this review, and no inter-rater reliability was measured. Each judgment is linked to hashes of both raw responses in temporal\_claim\_review.json. We do not report a hallucination or semantic-error rate.

\textbf{Stop this pilot branch here.} Neither the general shuffle diagnostic nor the observed text variation establishes a distinct learning contribution. No more temporal perturbation calls or prompt optimization are justified by this pilot. The original evidence-question direction remains conditional on accessible, independently verified support labels and a mechanism distinct from existing work. Annotation acquisition and source-grounded evaluation design are the next prerequisites; no additional GPU computation is scheduled from these findings. All frozen results remain preserved.

\textbf{Leakage hazard:} UCA's training split contains 214 VERA test videos. These are differing split conventions, not evidence that UCA's own splits leak. Any VERA learning experiment must exclude all VERA test IDs regardless of UCA labels; the 863-video joint training intersection is a candidate starting pool, subject to source/scene checks. Do not treat default UCA training as safe for VERA evaluation.

Timed captions provide external event references but are not exhaustive claim-support labels. Caption omission cannot establish a hallucination. Using them for training would introduce stronger supervision than original weak-label VERA and must be declared. No images or new model outputs were used in this metadata audit. Next assess annotation methodology and design a split-safe, explicitly caption-supervised question-learning comparison before fetching training videos or using GPUs. This reopens a conditional data route, not a novelty claim.


\section{Caption-supervision gate: annotation quality and rejected split}
UCA Section 3 and Appendix A describe ten trained annotators, a three-researcher review team, checks every 100 instances, and a final review. These establish a documented human-reference process, not exhaustive truth labels for arbitrary generated claims. Source: \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13925}.

VANGUARD already aligns UCA sentences to subclips, generates object-level reasoning and detector boxes, and trains LoRA adapters. It differs from frozen VERA question learning, but prevents claiming caption-informed reasoning or grounding alone as new. Source: \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02912v1}. We have not established the novelty of the narrower proposed feedback mechanism.

An ID-only candidate manifest was generated before images or predictions were examined: 16 joint-training videos and 12 joint-validation videos, selected by fixed SHA256 order. The latter contains ten normal videos and two burglaries; this cannot support a broad anomaly-category comparison. The manifest is retained as a rejected design artifact, not used for inference. Exact split-ID exclusion passed, but source-byte/scene independence has not been verified.

\textbf{Next design constraint:} preserve original VERA train/validation boundaries while considering UCA annotations across its alternative splits, rather than requiring both split names to agree. Define category coverage and positive-reference evaluation before selection. Caption absence remains unknown, not false. Compare classification-only feedback, no-sound/manual controls and caption-positive feedback at matched model-call budgets. A pilot can test feasibility; it cannot by itself establish novelty. No training videos fetched and no new GPU work performed.
